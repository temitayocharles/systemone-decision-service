# Employee Leave, PTO, Reimbursement, and Routine People-Operations Runbook

Use this runbook when an employee needs a clear answer about paid time off, leave requests, routine reimbursement, supporting documents, approval flow, or the status of an ordinary people-operations request. Local employment law, collective agreements, employment contracts, payroll rules, and the organization's formally approved policy remain authoritative.

## Operating principles

Give the employee the shortest accurate path to the next action. Separate policy from interpretation, and distinguish what the documentation says from what still requires a manager, HR, payroll, finance, or legal decision. Never invent eligibility, balances, deadlines, reimbursement limits, medical requirements, or approval authority. When a request falls outside the documented rules, route it to the appropriate human owner.

## Leave and PTO request flow

### 1. Identify the request type
Determine whether the employee is asking about vacation/PTO, sick leave, family or caregiving leave, bereavement, unpaid leave, statutory leave, jury or civic duty, medical accommodation, or another category.

### 2. Check the governing source
Use the current employee handbook, leave policy, employment agreement, union agreement where applicable, and jurisdiction-specific policy notes. If the sources disagree or appear outdated, do not choose a rule silently. Surface the conflict and escalate.

### 3. Confirm available balance when the system provides it
A policy can explain how balances work, but a knowledge assistant must not invent an employee's personal balance. Where balance information is not available in the approved source, say that the balance must be checked in the HR/payroll system.

### 4. Identify notice and documentation requirements
Explain the documented notice period, request channel, required attachments, and whether manager or HR approval is needed. Do not request sensitive medical detail beyond what the approved policy requires.

### 5. Submit through the approved channel
Direct the employee to the approved HR portal, form, email alias, manager workflow, or ticket path. State the expected acknowledgment or processing time only if the policy contains it.

### 6. Record exceptions
If staffing, blackout periods, overlapping leave, or statutory requirements create an exception, the assistant should summarize the policy and route the decision to a human owner rather than deciding the exception.

## Reimbursement flow

### 1. Identify the expense class
Examples include travel, meals, mileage, home-office purchases, tools, training, conference fees, customer expenses, and emergency purchases.

### 2. Verify pre-approval requirements
Some expense classes require approval before purchase. If the policy does not clearly state whether approval was required, do not retroactively invent a rule.

### 3. Verify required evidence
Typical evidence may include receipt, business purpose, date, amount, currency, project/client code, approver, or travel itinerary. Local policy controls.

### 4. Check submission timing
State the documented deadline. If no deadline is present in the approved source, say so and route the employee to finance/payroll for confirmation.

### 5. Explain the processing route
Describe where the claim is submitted and which team reviews it. Do not promise a payment date unless the approved policy provides one.

### 6. Handle rejected or partial claims
Explain the appeal or clarification path. Never characterize an expense as fraudulent or improper without evidence and human review.

## Questions the knowledge assistant should answer well

- How do I request PTO?
- What documents do I need for a reimbursement?
- Who approves a training expense?
- Where do I submit a mileage claim?
- What happens if my leave overlaps a busy period?
- Can I submit a missing receipt later?
- Which team handles an exception?

## Questions that require human or system lookup

- What is my current PTO balance?
- Will my manager approve these dates?
- Can an undocumented exception be granted?
- Is this medical situation legally protected?
- Has my reimbursement been paid?
- Can a policy requirement be waived?

## Escalation

Escalate when the request involves protected leave, accommodation, legal interpretation, discrimination or retaliation concerns, payroll disputes, suspected fraud, executive exceptions, policy conflicts, or sensitive personal information beyond the assistant's approved scope.

## Closure

A routine request is complete when the employee understands the applicable policy, knows the next action, has the correct submission path, and knows where to escalate if the documented process does not fit the situation.
