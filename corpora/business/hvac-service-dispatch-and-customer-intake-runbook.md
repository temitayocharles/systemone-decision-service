# HVAC Service Dispatch, Customer Intake, and Appointment Runbook

Use this runbook for residential or small-commercial HVAC inquiries involving service requests, scheduling, equipment information collection, maintenance questions, dispatch preparation, and non-technical customer updates. This runbook does not replace a qualified technician's diagnosis and does not instruct a customer to perform unsafe electrical, gas, refrigerant, combustion, or mechanical work.

## Operating principles

Collect enough information to route the job correctly, not enough to turn the customer into a technician. Distinguish emergency safety conditions from routine service. Keep commitments realistic and based on the live dispatch system.

## Intake

### 1. Capture the service address and safe contact method
Confirm the property location, caller name, callback number, and whether someone can provide site access.

### 2. Identify the equipment class
Record whether the issue involves furnace, boiler, heat pump, central air conditioning, mini-split, rooftop unit, thermostat, air handler, or another documented system.

### 3. Capture the symptom in the customer's words
Examples include no heat, no cooling, short cycling, unusual noise, water around equipment, thermostat blank, weak airflow, or system not starting.

### 4. Ask only approved safety-screening questions
If the customer reports gas smell, smoke, sparks, fire, suspected carbon monoxide, or another immediate hazard, direct them to the emergency/safety procedure and appropriate emergency services. Do not continue troubleshooting.

## Dispatch classification

### Routine
Maintenance, inspection, non-urgent performance concerns, filter/service reminders, quote requests.

### Priority
Loss of heating or cooling where weather or occupant circumstances materially increase impact, repeated equipment shutdown, water leakage with property risk, or documented priority-client conditions.

### Emergency/safety
Gas odor, smoke/fire, suspected carbon monoxide, exposed live electrical hazard, or other conditions covered by the emergency policy.

The live dispatch board, technician coverage, geography, and service agreements determine actual priority and arrival time.

## Scheduling

Offer only availability returned by the scheduling system. Explain service windows, access requirements, diagnostic fees, deposits, or cancellation rules only from current approved policy.

## Pre-arrival information

Where applicable, ask the customer to make the equipment area accessible, secure pets, identify the electrical panel or equipment location for the technician, and have prior service information available. Do not instruct the customer to open sealed equipment or defeat safety controls.

## Customer updates

If a technician is delayed, communicate the current dispatch status without inventing an arrival time. If the schedule materially changes, offer the approved rescheduling or escalation path.

## Questions the knowledge assistant should answer well

- What information do you need to book a service call?
- Do you service heat pumps?
- What should I do before the technician arrives?
- How do I reschedule?
- Is routine maintenance different from a repair call?
- What happens after I submit a service request?

## Questions that require a technician or human dispatcher

- What exactly is broken?
- How much will the final repair cost before diagnosis?
- Is it safe to bypass this switch?
- Can I handle refrigerant myself?
- Can you guarantee an arrival time not shown by dispatch?
- Can you waive a fee or service-area rule?

## Closure

The intake is complete when the customer is safely classified, the right information has been captured, a valid booking or escalation path has been provided, and no unsafe troubleshooting has been suggested.
